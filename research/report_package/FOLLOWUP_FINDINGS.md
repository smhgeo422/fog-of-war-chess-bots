# Game-derived follow-up · Results

The post-Stage-7 batch tested whether the existing findings survive contact with later positions from actual local games. Both bots used their pinned native components. eBotI played frozen v4 in three color-swapped seed pairs at 24+0.1, capped at 100 plies. All six runs were valid; five ended with a capturable king and one reached the cap. Default eBotI scored 3–1–2. Three pairs and a capped short-clock schedule are too little evidence to rank the bots. The opt-in feature stayed off in collection.

| Question | Result | Disposition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **P3. Does considering a missed reply change the move?** | Seven of 51 scored positions contained at least one slot replacement. Thirteen of 438 candidate-action/world combinations changed their reply set. Two of 53 scheduled positions lacked common engine tables. On the first exposed and ordinary eligible position per game, 11 matched complete comparisons changed zero root moves, with zero paired change in truncated reference regret. Across these evaluations, legacy and opt-in leaf tables accounted for 790,743 and 774,148 nodes. | The additional threats occurred in actual game histories and did not change decisions in the tested sample. The shared tables match nonterminal leaf counts and fixed depths. They leave live time and competitive strength unmeasured. Close the tested variant. |
| **P1. Does the proposal estimate real danger better?** | The scheduled screen missed all four positive labels. In the exploratory all-plies audit, 428 decisions had compatible retained support, 39 had nonzero estimated risk, and four labels were positive. At 64 draws, threat-biased sampling reduced squared error versus the retained-support mean from 0.00008910 to 0.00004811. Brier versus the real replay label was slightly worse, 0.006906 versus 0.006791. | More accurate sampling of the retained distribution does not fix errors in that distribution. The proposal checks the attack indicator across the retained support, and four positives across three pairs provide limited calibration evidence. |
| **P5. Does the frozen risk model transfer?** | There were 14 realized unseen undefended-piece losses after 422 actions with an observed reply. Only one occurred after the 295 noncapture actions in the primary comparison. The unchanged model's game-mean Brier was 0.004399 versus 0.003721 for the training-rate constant. The difference was adverse in all three color pairs. | No transfer signal for the current model. The primary outcome is very sparse here; this supports leaving the model experimental, not a general negative claim about risk prediction. |
| **P9. Does the tested endgame occur?** | Zero king-and-queen versus king positions occurred over 428 playable plies. | These reference games provide zero deployment coverage for that domain. The bounded soundness result remains separate. |

The report can now distinguish three independent failure points. Better sampling can reduce estimator error while leaving the belief wrong. A selector can examine more hidden danger while leaving the selected move unchanged. A risk predictor can fail to transfer as its target becomes sparse. All three outcomes are bounded by the available replay conditions. The evidence does not warrant tuning against this reused slice or advancing a new candidate to full-clock confirmation. P2 opponent adaptation, P4 live value of information, P6 deep restricted solving, P7 counterfactual value and P8 live clock allocation still need distinct data or integration prerequisites.
