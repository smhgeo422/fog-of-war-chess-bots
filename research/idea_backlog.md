# Fog of War Chess: literature review and research experiment backlog

**Prepared 2026-11-22.** Research proposals, not claims of completed implementations. Repository reference: https://github.com/smhgeo422/fog-of-war-chess-bots (upstream https://github.com/ernjg/fog-of-war-chess-bots). The Codex infrastructure branch was running at the time of research and its uncommitted changes were not audited. Never change frozen bot folders or publish a team token.

## Executive thesis

The central opportunity is to reconcile a good perfect-information tactical evaluator with the constraints of sequential imperfect information. Obscuro's advantage is not raw Stockfish evaluation; it is belief-state subgame construction with KLUSS, policy continuity across turns, predictive CFR+, selective game-tree expansion, and disciplined strategy purification. The team's v4/eBotI family is strongest when it deeply evaluates a weighted set of boards; prior larger KLUSS/GT-CFR implementations did not win locally, potentially because their evaluation was shallower and their solver budget inadequate. This motivates **targeted, information-aware correction**, not merely adding deeper/wider/safer determinization.

A second opportunity is statistical: replay analyses reveal substantial hidden-material losses on quiet moves, despite the truth usually being present in the belief set. The main weaknesses seem to be posterior mass distribution, action evaluation under future blindness, and the tradeoff between survival and attack. Causal hypotheses should be separated and tested. The project's limited compute and timeline require fast screening, then large independent matches for finalists.

## A. Primary sources and interpretation

## C. The core experimental design

Define controls B0=frozen `obscuro_v4`, B1=`eBotI` made v4-equivalent as far as possible (verified), B2=current experimental eBotI profile, and B3=the best validated combination. Run *one-feature* interventions against B1 first, followed by interactions between successful features. No feature gains credit for improvements due to a different clock, opening book, Stockfish version, CPU allocation, or random seed policy.

**Cheap evaluation**: unit tests; truth-board inclusion/weight rank on held-out replays; log likelihood of the actual opponent move; calibration of predicted immediate and 2–5-ply hazards; paired counterfactual move values; per-decision action divergence; compute overhead. Offline true boards are only **labels for analysis**, not bot inputs.

**Medium evaluation**: paired-seed or controlled opening matches, alternating colors, against v4, previous eBotI, greedy, random, and diverse local opponent styles. Short controls may screen regressions, but tournament matches under non-arena time limits are not final evidence. Record hardware CPU quotas and worker overlap.

**Confirmatory**: 180+2 time controls on isolated comparable machines; paired colors and both listing orders; precommitted configuration; game-level uncertainty intervals. For independent games with score rate near 50%, 120 games still have rough 95% error about ±9 percentage points; small improvements require many hundreds of games, with correlations accounted for. Bootstrap at matched-game or opening-block level; never count thousands of correlated moves as thousands of independent games. A final holdout opponent set must be kept separate from replay development.

**Recording**: hypothesis ID, exact commit SHA, full environment variables, binary SHA, side, clock settings, CPU, seeds, opponent, path, W/D/L, termination, 2/5-ply material/king risks, wall time, errors, evidence file hashes, confidence interval, and human interpretation. Guard against timeout artifacts and workers left after canceled tournaments. Only validation-passing bots should be considered for the arena.

## D. Experiment backlog

Notation: **S** small (hours), **M** medium (1–2 days), **L** large (several days), **XL** research-scale. These are effort categories, not promises of execution time. **Existing** means partly implemented in repo; **new** means research proposal; **revision** is a different mechanism than an already tried variant.

### A — Correct decisions under partial observation

**A01. Finish two-ply observation-consistent search (M; existing).** Theory: perfect-info search implicitly makes the next action depend on the hidden state, even where our next observation is identical. For action m, hypothetical reply r, board s, and resulting observation o, evaluate `sum_o P(o|m) max_a E[Q(s,m,r,a) | o,m]`, rather than `E[max_a Q(s,m,r,a)]`. The difference is a strategy-fusion/clairvoyance gap. Implement: use `eBotI/infoset.py` but add exact response legality, grouping-key tests, edge cases and explicit runtime fallback; sweep top candidates 3/5/8, states 8/12/24, opponent replies 2/3/5 and time share 0.2–0.6. Diagnose by whether selected actions are later invalid or attacked in indistinguishable scenarios; held-out 2–5-ply losses and match strength. It is important to **complete the existing experiment**, not claim credit for reimplementing it.

**A02. Information-consistent 3–5-ply tactical microsearch (L; new).** Limit search to 2–4 candidate moves and strategically important branches (king threats, loose valuable pieces, forced captures, knight forks). Alternate opponent actions and *our* observation-conditioned responses, not fully informed moves. Use top-K reply proposals from opponent policy; aggregate opponent response by appropriate information sets. Compare two-ply vs selective depth-3/4 under identical CPU. This attacks fatal errors that occur 2–5 plies after a seemingly good move. Failure risk: the branch factor and partial-observation legality model may overwhelm Stockfish depth.

**A03. Decision-focused value of information (M; new).** Instead of rewarding the number of visible squares or raw posterior entropy reduction, compute approximate `VOI(m)=E_o[max_a E(V(s,a)|o,m)]-max_a E(V(s,a)|m)` using a cheap continuation evaluator, excluding direct material benefits to avoid double count. An illuminating queen/rook move helps only if the information changes a future decision. Test `value + beta*VOI`, beta 0/0.1/0.3/0.6, and track actual observation gain, decision changes, and wins. Do not equate entropy with strategic value.

**A04. Information denial / opponent sightline model (M–L; new).** For each candidate simulated state, calculate squares and own pieces visible to the opponent after our move. Score important *newly revealed* own pieces and threats, comparing defensive tactics at the same material value. Model opponent observation using a hypothetical true state; average over our belief, and ideally over their inferred view history. Test on material-equivalent moves and replay counterfactuals. Avoid blindly penalizing all reveals: some force a tactically favorable response.

**A05. Conditional search by uncertainty and tactical motif (M; new).** Use only one-ply Stockfish in well-observed, low-threat positions, but trigger information-aware search when high hidden king/material risk, high move disagreement across sampled boards, or expected opponent revelation. Instrument fraction of decisions triggering, CPU spent, regret/win rate by stratum. Match compute against uniformly deeper search. Valuable if it reallocates limited runtime to the positions where information matters.

**A06. Small KLUSS-style local game, initialized by deep Stockfish (L; revision).** Build a restricted game involving only 3–6 candidate actions, 8–32 belief roots and several plausible opponent views, not a million-node replacement. Initialize leaf values with cached deep Stockfish scores and solve conditional action consistency with regret matching or tiny sequence-form solver. Include a small opponent-information layer (2-KLUSS spirit) only where its ambiguity can affect play. Ablate info layer, leaf strength, and solver iterations. Goal is to retain v4's tactical strength while buying genuine bluff/information awareness; prior full-tree GT-CFR failures motivate restriction.

**A07. Search-tree persistence and next-turn reuse (L; new architecture).** Obscuro carries its prior search tree and strategies between moves; independently reconstructed trees throw away information and compute. Persist a compact tree keyed by action-observation histories, retain surviving branches after observing the opponent response, renormalize weights, and reuse cached leaf scores/strategies. Measure reuse hits, saved engine evaluations, current-turn decision quality and memory usage. Cache keys must include castling/en-passant rights, side to move, half-move/repetition context and observable history when relevant; identical piece layouts alone can be unsound.

**A08. Revisit one-sided GT-CFR as a bounded subroutine (L; revision).** The repo's large growing-tree attempt lost short matches. Evaluate a *bounded* 2–4-ply game with Stockfish-based leaf scores and reuse, comparing one-sided vs two-sided expansion and frozen vs unfrozen second-order nodes. Run solver sanity tests on constructed toy imperfect-information games whose exact equilibrium is known before full FoW tests. Measure Nash gap, tree nodes per second, and whether the variant changes root decisions where a payoff-relevant bluff exists. A genuine negative finding at controlled budget is still useful.

### B — Better belief states and posterior sampling

**B01. Belief accuracy / calibration audit (S–M; new diagnostic).** On held-out arena replays, reconstruct beliefs without leaking true hidden state; measure true-state inclusion, probability rank, normalized log probability where available, ESS, king-attack calibration, dangerous-piece marginal calibration, and recall in top-K sampled roots by game phase/opponent. Separate (a) truth absent due to cap, (b) present but low probability, (c) correctly weighted but evaluated badly. This becomes the error decomposition that decides which algorithms are worth building.

**B02. Opponent-specific likelihood model (L; new).** Public replays identify opponent teams and historical moves. Fit `pi_theta(a | hypothetical board, partial-history features, opponent ID)` or a mixture of lightweight style experts, with a uniform floor and Bayesian shrinkage toward the current Stockfish-1-ply proposal. Features: move score rank, captures, threats, king exposure, development, phase, opponent Elo/identity. Weight successor states by policy likelihood conditioned on observed changes. Cross-validate on held-out games/time and evaluate true-state rank and next hidden-threat calibration before play. Critical: use identity-conditioned models only when known in actual bot protocol; if not, use latent style inference. Avoid overfitting a single opponent or inspecting data not allowed by arena policies.

**B03. Forward-simulated opponent information rather than a 'blind' board (L; revision).** Team's prior opponent model on a board with our unseen pieces removed performed worse than full-board Stockfish likelihood. That was not a faithful model of what the opponent knows. Maintain a distribution or representative particles over the opponent's plausible observations and piece-location beliefs; evaluate their candidate actions under *their* uncertainty, conditional on our hypothesized history. Compare predictive likelihood and action quality vs the blind-board and full-board models. High computational burden; use only a few opponent-view particles or selected critical turns.

**B04. Resampling, cap repair and particle rejuvenation (M–L; new).** The true board can fall out when position count reaches 200k. Add weighted reservoir sampling with explicit inclusion probabilities, stratify by material/tactical threat families, and a history-consistent rejuvenation process when observations prune too aggressively. Potential proposals: recent opponent moves reconstructed backward, local piece reassignment subject to legal histories, MCMC transition moves with observation acceptance tests. Report true-state retention, diversity, ESS and correctness against tiny exact-enumeration instances. Do not permit impossible boards or use the true board as an in-game oracle.

**B05. Danger-focused, importance-corrected root sampling (M; new).** Weighted sampling of just 64 roots can miss relevant attacks. Form an explicit proposal `q(s)=(1-alpha)b(s)+alpha q_threat(s)` where threat sampler emphasizes positions that attack our king/valuable pieces. Correct estimates by `b(s)/q(s)` weights, cap extreme importance weights and record effective sample size. Compare against uniform added breadth at *fixed Stockfish call count*. Rank by action-choice stability and rare-event recall, not coverage of all possible boards.

**B06. Learned piece-location marginals (M–L; new).** Train small phase-/observation-conditioned logits for `P(enemy piece on square | history features)` using simulated or permitted public replays. Respect conservation of enemy pieces and occupied/visible squares; don't independently sample piece locations without a legal-history checker. Blend marginal priors into particle proposals or hidden-attack hazard, then test expected attack probability calibration and material-loss outcomes. Start with logistic/gradient-boosted or shallow MLP models that can be deployed on CPU without new runtime dependencies.

**B07. Robust ensemble posterior, not a uniform fear penalty (M; new).** Maintain an ensemble of plausible likelihood temperatures, opponent styles, and particle-sampling schemes. For candidate action m report per-model values and event risk. Explore `E_model[V_m]` or a small *action-dependent disagreement penalty*, not naive CVaR over all boards (already failed). Test on held-out opponents and distribution shift; report when ensemble disagreement predicts bad decisions. Does combining models improve robustness without passivity?

**B08. Opponent policy via online latent-style adaptation (M–L; new).** Instead of a fixed known-opponent label, update weights on a handful of policies (Stockfish-tempered, capture-seeking, king-hunting, conservative, opening-book) from observed moves. Use a Bayesian mixture with forgetfulness/hierarchical prior so one surprising move cannot flip the posterior. Compare log-likelihood and game score vs single existing opponent model. Identifiability is weak under sparse observations, so use conservative updates.

### C — King safety, material preservation and tactical discipline

**C01. Controlled ablation of existing `FOW_HANG` (S–M; existing).** Run `lambda=0,0.25,0.5,1,2` *on v4-equivalent controls* to isolate an expected unseen-material capture penalty. Split captures vs quiet moves; record loss of moved piece vs standing pieces and opponent reply capture rates. Include the important capture-exempt variant, already used in code, against a naive all-moves-penalized control to demonstrate why captured material can justify exposure. Avoid claiming causality from the 9.27 vs 7.78 points/game observational comparison.

**C02. View-only geometric exposure threshold (S–M; existing foundation / new switch).** Compute, after quiet action, a calibrated exposure score based on unseen rook/bishop/knight/pawn attack origins, visible blockers, remaining opponent pieces, and defense status. Reject high-exposure quiet moves only when a materially comparable low-exposure alternative exists; sweep thresholds and a *soft* penalty. Compare `FOW_FOG_TAX` to exact belief `FOW_HANG` and their combination. Test whether low-risk moves give up initiative; do not penalize captures globally.

**C03. Two- and three-move material danger forecast (M–L; new).** Model `P(valuable piece captured within k plies | m)` under opponent policy and our observation-constrained reply for k=2/3, optionally only for top move candidates. This is distinct from the existing one-ply `hang` helper and should catch exposed pieces that are not yet under attack but become targets after rook swings/open lines. Use rare-event simulation or a motif-limited tree to stay cheap. Assess on held-out survival curves and direct head-to-head games, with direct material gain separated from unexplained exposure.

**C04. Attack probability times realistic capture propensity (M; new).** `FOW_HANG` counts or prices possible captures; not every attacked piece is actually taken immediately, and defenders or more valuable threats affect opponent preference. Train/estimate `P(opponent captures piece | attacked, visibility, relative exchange value, opponent style)` and multiply by expected SEE/net exchange loss, distinguishing protected vs free pieces. Compare Brier/log loss on held-out replays and actual game-strength gain vs a flat hazard penalty. Avoid using attacker-visible status backwards: it is our visibility of the attacker that determines whether the hazard was hidden.

**C05. King-risk as win probability, not centipawn exchange (M; revision).** Current formula `-risk + (1-risk)*mean` mixes immediate terminal loss with tanh-transformed Stockfish centipawn value. Test calibrating conditional `P(win|centipawns, phase, uncertainty, time)` from local games and combine `(1-p_lost) E[V_win] - p_lost`. Nonlinear value should distinguish 'down a rook but king safe' from 'dead king'. Compare replays and games, especially hopeless-looking but winnable FoW positions. Prior global `FOW_RISK_LAMBDA=5` failed; this changes the *value scale*, not merely multiplying risk.

**C06. Zero-risk-first, but conditional on action quality (S–M; existing variant).** `FOW_SAFE_FIRST=1` is already in eBotI. Test strict lexicographic 0-risk vs penalized risk vs opportunity-aware selection, with safeguards against known forced losses or severe material sacrifice. Distinguish true exact zero risk in enumerated set from zero estimated under a capped/misspecified belief. Evaluate on the subset where at least one zero-risk move exists and on the full arena; one subset is not enough.

**C07. Unseen-check escape policy / anti-hunt defense (M; new).** Prior losses include standing in unseen check or moving king into fog attack. When sample risk indicates hidden check, prioritize blocking/capture/king escape that is safe across candidate states, and prevent enemy rook/queen control of long lanes. Implement limited forced-line solving for king-safety motifs and expose a diagnostic 'escape options remain' metric. Test against specially constructed rook/queen hunt scenarios; avoid a blanket king-move penalty (already failed).

**C08. Active king-hunting as a risk-conditioned strategy (M; new).** FoW rewards capturing the king, including checks the opponent cannot see. Generate high-probability attack sequences against enemy king marginals, with accurate terminal and forced-escape rules. Use opportunistic mixed threats when materially behind and standard play when ahead. Test actual king captures, time-to-convert, material sacrificed, and opponent responses; distinguish a coherent plan from bonus points for every check (previous check boosts failed in belief weighting).

### D — Game-theoretic reasoning, bluffing and opponent exploitation

**D01. Safe tactical bluff score (M–L; new).** For a candidate move, compare what our move accomplishes under the true-hypothesized board with how dangerous it appears *to the opponent given its view*. Example: leave a defended piece apparently undefended, or conceal a queen/rook attack. Approximate opponent's likelihood of accepting bait and actual payoff when called. Test targeted local positions with and without an opponent-view model. Threats should be valued only when opponent uncertainty makes a response difference; never blindly reward hanging pieces.

**D02. Restricted root security game (M; new).** Solve a 3–8 by 3–8 matrix between our candidate moves and opponent reply templates aggregated over beliefs. Mix policies when pure play is exploitable. Use full-game value/king-risk from deeper evaluator and evaluate equilibrium gaps on the *restricted game*, not claim global equilibrium. Distinguish minimax, opponent-model response, and weighted hybrid. Tests against a deliberate exploiter (that knows our policy) and ordinary arena-like bots quantify the robustness/exploitation tradeoff.

**D03. Strategy purification and stable mixing (S–M; new to Stockfish path).** The paper's policy chooses from at most three stable actions and only mixes when its subgame result is safe. Our Stockfish path has `FOW_RANDOM` but no true PCFR+-based stability criterion. Explore three candidate gates: close value margin, stable best-action under root resampling, and small max regret under restricted game. Randomize only within this filtered set, compare deterministic top vs uniform near-top vs softmax vs purified. Evaluate head-to-head and exploitability proxies against replay-adaptive opponent. Mixing can lose rating against unadaptive bots, so assess both.

**D04. Resolve/Maxmargin switch and opponent root priors (L; paper-derived revision).** Within a small subgame experiment, carry a blueprint from last move, construct Resolve and Maxmargin alternate-value gadgets, implement paper's half-uniform/half-prior opponent-root mixture, and switch when margins become unsafe. Test synthetic games for monotonic safety constraints and FoW positions for tactical consistency; compare uniform roots and no inherited blueprint. Relevant only once a real, persistent game-tree solver exists.

**D05. PCFR+ update schedule and convergence diagnostics (M; solver optimization).** Compare last iterate, reach-weighted average, purification, warm start, and 2026 scheduling variants on the *same restricted game* and equal iteration/time budget. Compute Nash gap or exploitability exactly on small toy subgames, and root action stability on FoW. A fast solver that produces unstable last iterates should not drive moves; choose scheduling based on empirical objective rather than paper prestige.

**D06. Robust policy versus opponent-specific best response (M–L; new).** Use a trusted baseline policy (deep Stockfish or equilibrium restricted-game) and an exploitative action value under learned opponent style. Blend or select using uncertainty in the opponent model; do not use exploitation when style confidence is low. Evaluate against two populations: fixed deterministic bots and policy-aware adversaries. Obscuro intentionally does not model opponents; this is a defensible departure with a clear scientific hypothesis.

**D07. Opponent population / PSRO-lite (L–XL; new).** Keep a diverse suite of bots or configs (greedy, v4, defensive, king-hunter, high-variance, our candidates). Compute cross-play matrix and train a meta-strategy over candidate policies, with a simple response-oracle loop that adds a new specialist only if it improves the mixture. Start with a *selection/mixture* experiment before full RL. Measure worst-opponent score and average across population, and report nontransitivity. Avoid basing progress solely on one v4 head-to-head match.

### E — Learning, self-play and data-driven evaluation

**E01. Supervised next-ply fog hazard model (M; new).** Predict `P(hidden capture or king attack within one opponent move | observation and candidate action)` from replay labels, using geometric features, belief features, piece values and phase. Start with interpretable logistic regression or gradient-boosted trees. Calibrate with reliability plots and held-out games, then add the predicted *residual* hazard to Stockfish. Compare vs hand-built exposure and exact belief helper to avoid paying twice for risks already priced.

**E02. Lightweight FoW-specific value residual (L; new).** On replayed candidate positions, train a residual to correct deep Stockfish's perfect-information score for hidden-information outcomes, not replace chess evaluation. Features: king-visibility, posterior entropy, quiet material exposure, opponent information advantage, threats, game phase. Targets could be future k-ply realized material/king outcomes or game outcome using out-of-sample self-play; control for selection bias (played actions differ from unplayed). Deploy as small linear or neural model using CPU-safe inference. Main question: does correcting value bias help more than expensive deeper search?

**E03. Distill expensive information-set search (L; new).** Generate high-quality teacher decisions using offline deep observation-consistent planning/GT-CFR on sampled states. Train a small student to predict teacher move ranking or value correction from current observation and compressed belief features. At tournament time the student chooses whether to invoke costly refinement and/or supplies prior scores; goal: obtain 70–90% of information-aware benefit at a fraction of runtime. Evaluate teacher agreement, actual regret and matches against equally budgeted Stockfish baseline.

**E04. Learned state proposal distribution (L; new).** Train a conditional hidden-board generator or constrained autoregressive piece placement proposal, with legal-state filtering through known observations. Supervise on local simulated true boards; validate recall of rare attacks and posterior calibration. Initially use simple independent-marginal/proposal + correction model; a diffusion model is a stretch, since running it on two CPUs plus Stockfish may be prohibitive. Never use an unconstrained generated board as an actual belief state without checking consistency.

**E05. Opponent-policy imitation from replay corpus (M–L; new).** Predict moves from observation/history *as available to the opponent*, with identities or style clusters. Contrast 1-ply full-board Stockfish likelihood, observed-board-only heuristic, and full multi-hypothesis belief. Success criteria: improvement in held-out move log-likelihood and posterior concentration, not just imitation accuracy. Distinguish opponent choice model from adversarial equilibrium policy.

**E06. Search-quality teacher and compute scheduler (M–L; new).** On a sample of positions, run several expensive configurations offline to estimate which positions benefit from more belief roots, deeper Stockfish, or information-set search. Fit a low-cost classifier/regressor to allocate time and choose algorithm per turn. Verify average improvement under equal total CPU and clock; guard against gaming the clock and endgame timeouts.

**E07. End-to-end RL / PSRO deep self-play (XL; research stretch).** Train policy/value networks with legal-action masking, observation histories and recurrent or transformer state, using a fast parallel simulator; bootstrap from Stockfish expert games to avoid poor sample efficiency. Consider PSRO/regularized policy-gradient approaches inspired by DeepNash and Ataraxos. This is ambitious but likely bottlenecked by no GPU at play, data generation and time before the Dec 11 deadline. A modest pilot with supervised imitation and self-play fine-tuning is the realistic first experiment; full tabula-rasa performance claims require far more evidence.

### F — Reliable chess strength and decision-time compute

**F01. Opening protocol / repertoire (S–M; existing book + extension).** Existing `book_gg.json` samples Greek Gift's replay choices. Build a principled small *observation-keyed* opening book based on Obscuro's high-level first-move distribution (White mostly d4/c4; Black defends the a4–e8 diagonal), plus empirical survival/benefit. Ab late vs early book exit, uniform vs purified randomized openings, and generic vs opponent-specific responses. Prevent book leakage of hidden positions and illegal moves. Avoid using the original paper's opening distribution as if it were necessarily optimal for this course's opponent pool.

**F02. Clock allocation with survival reserve (S; existing).** `FOW_CLOCK=reserve` is already present; compare vs v4 rule at 180+2 and short screening time controls, controlling for algorithm changes. Measure engine depth, spend distribution by phase, remaining time at finish, and flag losses. Adapt later to hazard or uncertainty only if the reserve rule alone improves play. Do not infer an Elo improvement solely from thinking longer, nor overload worker CPUs.

**F03. Adaptive state count vs Stockfish depth (M; new).** Pick breadth using posterior ESS, top candidate disagreement, uncertainty about risk and available budget. Deepen top candidates when many sampled states agree, broaden when posterior supports widely different tactics. Maintain minimum danger sample/rare-event branch. Compare against fixed 16/32/64/128 roots at fixed total node evaluations, track actual engine search depths and action stability; team previously found 384 naive roots often harmful.

**F04. Progressive candidate pruning and correlated evaluation (M–L; new).** Instead of asking MultiPV for every move on all roots, estimate value intervals for candidate moves from a small shared root sample, prune clearly dominated actions, then spend remaining compute on uncertain top moves. Use common random state samples for each action to reduce comparison variance and conservative confidence bounds to avoid deleting rare king defenses. Measure compute vs top-action agreement with expensive offline oracle and game results.

**F05. Special-case terminal/endgame solver (M; new).** When the board is highly simplified, Stockfish's chess checkmate/draw assumptions can be incorrect in FoW: the king can be captured, ordinary legal king-in-check restrictions differ, and even K-vs-K cases are strategically nontrivial. Build a rules-faithful small-state endgame solver or table for few remaining pieces, and selectively override Stockfish's misleading terminal scores. Test with synthetic edge-case boards, forced lines, actual late-game match outcomes and time-to-convert. Include repetition and 100-ply draw handling; do not assume chess endgame tablebases apply.

**F06. King chase and winning conversion without overplaying (M; new).** When ahead, tune between safe pressure, preserving visibility, and direct king hunting; avoid repeated-position draws and unproductive shuffles. Measure conversion rate from material-leading positions and median win length. When behind, permit higher-variance king-hunt threats if justified. The objective is eventual king capture, not simply material advantage.

**F07. CPU, packaging and protocol optimization (S–M; infrastructure).** Confirm 15-second startup, 5-minute network-free build, no unsafe stdout, 180+2 clock discipline, 2 cores, 4GiB, Stockfish and native-helper presence. Profile subprocess overhead, engine restarts, state-set update time and C++ allocations. Optimize bottlenecks only after profiling, with per-decision speed and matching strength, and verify that a better local result does not depend on non-arena AVX settings or oversubscription.

**F08. Hybrid portfolio arbitration (M–L; capstone).** Build a meta-decision engine combining a baseline deep-Stockfish score, a calibrated king/material hazard score, an information-consistent refinement, and optional small game-theoretic policy. Use confidence-weighted gating or a constrained mixture: when models agree, pick robust top; when one flags a catastrophe, escalate; when opponent-specific data is strong, use exploitative adjustment. Ablate every ingredient and all meaningful pairwise interactions. This is not a single new heuristic—it tests whether different corrections fix complementary errors.

## E. Suggested phase order (not a promise to run everything automatically)

**Phase 0, prerequisites**: complete Codex infra branch, persistent Git write/PR path, exact baseline parity, tournament isolation, valid match results and held-out replays. A task running successfully in Codex does not mean it can automatically persist to GitHub. The public arena beta and permissioned uploads must remain manual unless separately authorized.

**Phase 1, fast high-impact tests**: C01/C02/C06, F01/F02/F03, B01, A01 and F05. These primarily improve/measure existing code and yield clean ablations.

**Phase 2, structural value/belief advances**: A02/A03/A05/A06, B02/B04/B05, C03/C04/C05, E01. These are the main original engineering research contributions.

**Phase 3, game theory, representation and learning**: A04/A07/A08, B03/B06/B07/B08, D01–D07, E02–E07, F04/F06/F08. Try synthetic or offline pilots first; promote only viable implementations to full matches. Several are expensive research directions and may be impractical by the arena deadline.

**Phase 4, factorial + final report**: combine only successful independent features; measure interactions with at least a small 2×2 design; full 180+2 holdout matches; produce evidence table, counterexamples, negative results, runtime, statistical uncertainty, practical and theoretical caveats, AI-use disclosure. Submit with course-approved process; do not trust local ratings as the same as arena ratings.

## F. High-value report questions

- Does online value correction against future observational blindness outperform additional Stockfish depth at equal compute?
- Is the error primarily missing support (true board outside set), posterior miscalibration, or strategy fusion when future observations are shared?
- Is expected hidden *material* danger more actionable than catastrophe-only king-risk penalties, and how do we avoid double counting it?
- When does modeling the opponent's imperfect information buy strength, and when is a perfect-information opponent model a stronger effective predictor?
- Can restricted KLUSS/PCFR+ search capture bluffing/strategy robustness without the leaf-evaluation sacrifice of a large GT-CFR tree?
- Do selective information-search triggers offer greater strength per CPU second than uniform depth/breadth?
- Which changes improve win rate across heterogeneous opponents rather than overfitting one style? Are policy mixtures helpful only against adaptable opponents?
- Do learned value and belief components improve beyond transparent rules on out-of-distribution holdout games?

**Important research ethics:** Prior arena replays and statistical results are observational; distinguish hypothesis from proven causal improvement. A paper-derived algorithm is not ours; the report contribution is the adaptation, control, measured result, insight, and identified limits. Clearly document humans' and AI systems' roles.
